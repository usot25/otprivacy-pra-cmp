The white tiger is not a species or a subspecies. It is a Bengal tiger that inherited two copies of a rare recessive gene, which removes the orange from its coat and leaves the stripes.

Why the white tiger matters

Its white coat is beautiful, but it gives no camouflage in the forest. Almost every white tiger alive today was bred in captivity, often from close relatives.

Sources: National Geographic; WWF; IFAW.

What makes a tiger white

In 2013, researchers at Peking University traced the white coat to a single change in the gene SLC45A2. It blocks the red and yellow pigment and leaves the black.

Two copies, or none

The gene is recessive. A cub is white only if it inherits a copy from each parent. One normal copy makes it orange. In the wild this happens roughly once in 10,000 births.

Blue eyes, pink nose

White tigers usually have blue eyes and a pink nose. Like all tigers, each one has its own stripe pattern, and the stripes show on the skin even if the fur is shaved.

Full-sized Bengal

Males can reach about 260 kg and 3 m in length, and are fully grown at around three years old. Females are smaller, at up to about 170 kg.

White tigers are often called albino. They are not: albinism removes all pigment, while the white tiger still makes black pigment for its stripes and eyes.

From Rewa to the world’s zoos

How one cub became the ancestor of most captive white tigers.

  1. 1500s

    First records

    White tigers are recorded in India.

  2. 1951

    Mohan is captured

    Maharaja Martand Singh of Rewa takes a white male cub from the forests of present-day Madhya Pradesh. He names him Mohan and keeps him at Govindgarh Palace.

  3. 1958

    A first litter, and a last sighting

    Mohan is bred with his daughter Radha, and four white cubs are born in October. The same year, the last white tiger known in the wild is shot.

  4. 1969

    Mohan dies

    He dies aged about 19. His descendants have been sent to zoos in India and abroad.

  5. 2011

    Accredited zoos stop breeding

    The Association of Zoos and Aquariums bars its members from breeding for the white coat.

  6. 2013

    The gene is found

    Researchers identify SLC45A2 as the cause of the white coat.

  7. 2016

    A safari in Mukundpur

    The Maharaja Martand Singh Judeo White Tiger Safari opens near Rewa, where Mohan’s story began.

  8. 2022

    US law on big cats

    The Big Cat Public Safety Act ends private ownership of big cats as pets and bans public contact with cubs.

Where they were found, and where they are now

There are no white tigers known in the wild today. Every one you can see lives in a zoo, safari park or sanctuary.

Then: the wild

Rewa, Madhya Pradesh
The historic centre of white tiger records, and the forests where Mohan was found. The area is now part of the Sanjay-Dubri and Bandhavgarh landscape.
Elsewhere in India
Occasional reports from Assam, West Bengal, Bihar, Odisha and the Sundarbans.
Bengal tiger range
Had they survived, white tigers would share the Bengal tiger’s forests and wetlands in India, Bangladesh, Bhutan and Nepal.

Now: captivity

Zoos and sanctuaries
About 200 worldwide, roughly half of them in India.
Mukundpur, Madhya Pradesh
Home to the world’s first white tiger safari, opened in 2016 near Rewa.
United States
Many captive lines were crossed with Amur tigers in the 1970s, so most are not pure Bengal.

The cost of breeding for colour

Breeding for white means breeding relatives. Conservation groups say it does nothing for tigers in the wild.

Health

Inbred tigers can suffer spinal deformities, organ defects, immune problems and crossed eyes.

Survival

IFAW reports a study in which 80% of inbred cubs died shortly after birth.

Profit

Cubs draw paying visitors for photos and handling, which is the main reason many are bred.

Measures being taken

Protecting the white tiger means protecting wild Bengal tigers and their forests, and ending profit-driven breeding.

  • India Project Tiger now covers 53 reserves, and India holds about 75% of the world’s wild tigers Since 1973 (Government of India press release, opens in a new tab)
  • United States The Big Cat Public Safety Act restricts breeding, private ownership and cub handling Law, 2022 (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, opens in a new tab)
  • Zoos Accredited zoos no longer breed for the white coat Policy, 2011 (WWF, opens in a new tab)
  • Field work Discovery and WWF funded anti-poaching, monitoring and habitat protection through Project C.A.T. Partnership (Discovery, opens in a new tab)
